Second Species Counterpoint — Operational Guide

This guide establishes the precise constraints and logic required to generate or evaluate second-species counterpoint, following the principles of Gradus ad Parnassum.
I. Structural Framework
	1	Metric Ratio: 2:1. Two half notes (minims) in the counterpoint against one whole note (semibreve) in the Cantus Firmus (CF).
	2	Terminology: The first half note is the Thesis (downbeat); the second is the Arsis (upbeat).
	3	The Opening: The first measure may begin with a half rest on the Thesis. The first sounding note must be a perfect consonance (1, 5, or 8).
	4	The Conclusion: The final measure must always be a whole note (consonant unison or octave).
II. Intervallic Constraints
	1	Thesis (Downbeat) Rule: Every Thesis note must be strictly consonant (Unison, 3rd, 5th, 6th, 8th, or 10th). Dissonance is never permitted on the downbeat.
	2	Arsis (Upbeat) Rules:
	- 	Passing Dissonance: A dissonance is permitted only as a passing tone. It must be approached by step and left by step in the same direction (filling the space of a 3rd between two consonant downbeats).
	- 	Consonant Leap: If the Arsis note is approached or left by a leap (skip), that note must be consonant with the CF.
	3	Unison Restriction: Avoid the unison within the exercise; it is permitted only in the first and last measures.
III. Progression Logic 
Structural stability is determined by the relationship between successive downbeats. The upbeat does not always "clear" the path for the next downbeat.
	1	Successive Perfect Intervals: Parallel 5ths or 8ths occurring on consecutive downbeats are forbidden.
	2	The "Third" Limitation: An intervening note on the Arsis that is only a third away from the Thesis does not break the perception of a parallel. The ear "remembers" the first note.
	3	The Leap Rule: To successfully invalidate a potential parallel between two downbeats, the Arsis must move by a leap of a 4th, 5th, or 6th. This distance causes the ear to "forget" the previous structural note.
	4	Imperfect to Perfect: When moving from an imperfect consonance to a perfect consonance on consecutive downbeats, contrary motion is required unless a leap of a 4th or larger on the Arsis mitigates the direct succession.
IV. Formulas for the Cadences
The penultimate measure must follow these specific intervallic patterns to resolve to the final whole note.
A. Counterpoint Above the CF:
	- 	Measure (n-1): Thesis = 5th | Arsis = Major 6th
	- 	Final Measure: Whole note = Octave
B. Counterpoint Below the CF:
	- 	Measure (n-1): Thesis = 5th | Arsis = minor 3rd
	- 	Final Measure: Whole note = Unison (or Octave)
V. Strategic Exceptions and Planning
	1	Register Reset: If the voices become too close or contrary motion is impossible, use a leap of a minor 6th or an octave to reposition the counterpoint.
	2	Forward Planning: Each measure must be chosen not only for its own correctness but to facilitate a valid progression into the following two measures.
	3	Prohibited Intervals: Avoid all augmented and diminished intervals (e.g., the Tritone) in the melodic line, even if reached stepwise, unless the line continues correctly to resolve the tension.
VI. Summary Checklist for Generation
	-  Does every measure have a 2:1 ratio (except the last)?
	-  Is every Thesis note consonant?
	-  Is every dissonant Arsis a passing tone (approached/left by step)?
	-  Are there parallel 5ths/8ths between downbeats? (If yes, is the Arsis leap more or equal to a 4th?)
	-  Does the penultimate measure follow the 5-6 (above) or 5-3 (below) formula?
	-  Is the "Mi against Fa" (Tritone) avoided?
